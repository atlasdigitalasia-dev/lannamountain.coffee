Our story

Digital marketing built
from inside the industry.

We didn't learn coffee from a brief. We sourced it direct from farms, built a brand, met the farmers, and discovered firsthand why digital presence makes or breaks a coffee business.

The founder
"The best coffee businesses I've seen lose to weaker competitors — not because their product isn't better, but because nobody can find them online."

My name is Tom. I'm a digital marketer based in Chiang Rai, Northern Thailand — one of the most exciting specialty coffee origins in the world. But before I was a marketer, I was a coffee business owner.

I built Lanna Mountain Coffee — sourcing direct from smallholder farms in the hills around Chiang Rai and selling online in Thailand. I met the farmers. I understood their harvest cycles, their processing methods, their pride in what they grew. I learned how hard it is to translate that story into something that sells online.

The brand didn't make me rich. But it taught me something more valuable: I saw exactly where the gap was between exceptional coffee and the digital presence needed to sell it. Most small coffee businesses — roasters, importers, exporters — have incredible product and almost no online visibility.

That experience led me to digital marketing, and eventually to specialising in the coffee industry specifically. I've since built websites and digital strategies for around ten specialty roasters across New Zealand, Australia, and the UK — learning what works for this industry across very different markets.

What running a coffee brand taught us

The lessons that shaped
how we work.

Building and losing a coffee brand was the most useful education we could have had. Here's what it taught us about marketing in this industry.

🌱

Origin stories don't sell themselves

We had an exceptional origin story — direct from Chiang Rai farms, traceable, relationship-driven. But a great story buried on a mediocre website reaches nobody. The story has to be findable, structured, and told in the language buyers actually search for.

🔍

Visibility is a business decision

We were invisible online. Not because the product wasn't good enough — it was. Because we hadn't invested in being findable. Every coffee business we've worked with since has had the same core problem. Great product, weak digital presence.

🤝

Trade buyers research differently

Retail consumers browse. Trade buyers research. A roaster shortlisting importers, a cafe evaluating wholesale partners, an equipment buyer comparing options — they use different search behaviour, ask different questions, and need different content to feel confident. Generic marketing misses them entirely.

💡

Digital and coffee expertise rarely coexist

We spent time trying to find agencies who understood both. Most knew digital but had to learn coffee from scratch — which meant we spent half the engagement teaching them the industry. That gap is exactly why this service exists.

How we work

A few things we
believe strongly.

🎯

Specificity over generalism

A roaster's website is not the same as a retailer's website. An importer's SEO is not the same as a café's SEO. We refuse to apply generic templates to a specialist industry. Everything we build is specific to your business type, your buyers, and your market.

📊

Honesty over optimism

We don't over-promise results or use vanity metrics to obscure what's actually happening. Monthly reporting is clear, specific, and honest — including when something isn't working and needs to change.

🔗

Long-term over transactional

The coffee industry runs on relationships. We treat client work the same way — we want to understand your business deeply and build something that grows over time, not deliver a project and disappear.

🚀

Ahead of the curve

GEO and AI SEO aren't mainstream yet in the coffee industry. That's exactly why we lead with them. The businesses that establish AI visibility now will have a significant advantage over those who wait until it becomes standard practice.
